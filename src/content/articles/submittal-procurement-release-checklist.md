An approved submittal and a purchase release answer different questions. The first records the result of a review under the project's procedures. The second commits the business to an order, fabrication slot or delivery arrangement. A good release check connects the two before someone assumes that a favorable review stamp means everything is ready.

This workflow is a practical management approach. The contract, specifications and actual review comments determine the requirements for a particular package. Use those documents to decide who may release work and what conditions must first be satisfied.

## Give every release one identifiable package

Start with the item, specification section and exact revision. A supplier's quotation, reviewed drawing and purchase order can all describe the same equipment while referring to different models or options. Comparing filenames is not enough: verify the identifying information inside the documents.

Name the person assembling the release record and the person authorized to approve the purchasing decision. They can be the same person on a small project, but both responsibilities still need attention. Record the decision in a place the project team can find later.

Keep clarification and change records connected. If a review comment points to an unresolved RFI, the package may still depend on another decision. If a proposed variation affects the agreed scope, follow the contract's change procedure. Do not let a procurement deadline silently settle that question.

## Check six things before releasing fabrication

| Check | Evidence to inspect | Action if unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Item, model, specification section and current revision | Reconcile conflicting document references |
| Review result | Returned package and every review comment | Identify conditions that affect release |
| Coordination | Interfaces, dimensions and required field verification | Assign the missing check to a named person |
| Commercial scope | Purchase order, inclusions, exclusions and approved changes | Resolve the difference before committing |
| Timing | Confirmed production, delivery and installation dates | Recalculate the available schedule allowance |
| Distribution | Current package available to supplier and field team | Replace superseded copies and confirm receipt |

Treat this as an evidence checklist rather than a collection of yes/no boxes. “Approved” is incomplete when the actual return says to revise a connection detail before fabrication. Record the condition and its disposition.

For a specific federal-contract example, [FAR 52.236-21](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.236-21) addresses contractor coordination and review of shop drawings, the Contracting Officer's approval process and written identification of variations. It also explains that approval does not remove the contractor's stated responsibilities. Apply that clause where it belongs; do not assume it is the contract language for every private project.

## Work backward from the actual field need

Consider this illustrative sequence, expressed in working days. A crew needs a package ready for installation in 45 working days. The supplier currently estimates 25 working days for fabrication and delivery after release. The project team allows five working days for its own coordination, ten for external review and five for correcting the package.

Those allowances consume all 45 working days. There is no spare allowance for an additional review cycle. If the supplier's confirmed lead time grows by five working days, the installation plan is now exposed even if no submittal due date has yet been missed.

The useful decision is to identify a feasible response: bring forward a design decision, confirm whether a partial release is contractually and technically appropriate, adjust the installation sequence, or investigate an acceptable alternative. Do not simply change the forecast date while leaving the reason unrecorded.

Use the same working calendar throughout the calculation. A supplier's “five weeks” may not mean 25 project working days when holidays, shutdowns or delivery restrictions apply. Ask for a dated commitment and document the assumptions.

## Close the loop after the release

Send the released revision to the supplier and affected field team. Record what was sent, when it was sent and who acknowledged it. Remove or clearly supersede earlier working copies so a later crew does not install from an obsolete detail.

A release is not the end of procurement tracking. Continue monitoring production, delivery arrangements, storage constraints and changes. When a forecast moves, connect it to the installation activity and the person responsible for responding.

At the next coordination meeting, review exceptions rather than reading every row of the register. Discuss packages with unresolved conditions, changed lead times or missing field information. A short exception list is useful when its entries identify an owner, a decision and a due date.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a submittal register?

A submittal register lists the items the specifications require, with an identifier, responsible party and planned dates. Build it from the contract documents before packages start arriving. Add submission, review and revision status as work progresses so missing items remain visible.

### How to create a submittal?

Identify the applicable specification section, assemble the required drawings, product information or samples, and check the package against the project requirements. Use a cover sheet identifying the project, item and revision. Clearly flag proposed deviations, then send the coordinated package through the agreed review route.

### What are some examples of submittals?

Examples include equipment product data, coordinated fabrication drawings, finish samples, mock-ups, test reports and operation manuals. Each serves a different purpose: a sample can establish an accepted finish, while a shop drawing can show dimensions and connections. The specifications determine what the project requires.

### What are the three main reasons submittals are rejected?

There is no universal ranking of three reasons. Three useful checks are whether the package is complete, whether the proposed product or detail matches the specified requirement, and whether dimensions and interfaces are coordinated. Read the actual review comments before deciding what needs correction.

### What does a submittal schedule look like?

Use one row per required package, showing its specification section, supplier, review duration, planned submission date, required approval date and linked installation activity. Include time for correction and resubmission. Keep the current forecast beside the original target so changes in procurement timing are visible.

### What is a submittal vs RFI?

An RFI asks for missing information or clarification; a submittal presents the product, material or detail proposed for the work. A submittal review can reveal a question that needs an RFI. Track the connection, while following the contract's separate process for changes in price, time or scope.

### What is a technical submittal?

A technical submittal presents information needed to assess a proposed product, system or detail against project requirements. It might contain performance data, drawings, test information or calculations requested by the specifications. Its required contents and reviewer depend on the item and the contract.

### Who approves submittals?

Follow the review responsibilities in the contract. Contractors generally coordinate and review their packages before sending them to the designated reviewer. On federal work using FAR 52.236-21, the Contracting Officer indicates shop-drawing approval or disapproval; that approval does not remove the contractor's stated responsibilities.
